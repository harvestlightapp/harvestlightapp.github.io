Frequently asked questions

Shooting hours alarms — your phone stays in your pocket, you stay ready.

Do I need to create an account?

No. HarvestLight has no login or sign-up of any kind. If you're signed in to iCloud on your device, your dates and alarms sync privately across your own devices through your own iCloud account — there's nothing to set up beyond that.

How do the alarms work?

Each date can have a start alarm and an end alarm, timed however you like relative to that day's shooting hours. Pick a vibration pattern once (Single, Double, or Triple buzz, or one of the two repeated-group patterns) and it's used for every alarm. Alarms are scheduled entirely on your device — HarvestLight has no server and sends nothing over the network to trigger them.

Will an alarm go off if my phone is on silent or in Focus/Do Not Disturb?

Alarms use iOS's Time-Sensitive notification level, so they're able to break through Focus and Do Not Disturb. Put your phone on silent rather than fully powering it off or force-quitting the app — HarvestLight is designed to be felt as a vibration with the phone in your pocket, not watched on screen.

Why does an alarm pop up even while I'm actively using the app?

This is intentional. A shooting-hours alarm is exactly the kind of thing that shouldn't get silently swallowed just because the app happened to be open at the time — so HarvestLight always shows the full alert: banner, sound, and vibration alike. If the banner itself feels like too much, you can soften how it's displayed (without affecting the sound or vibration) from Settings → Notifications → HarvestLight → Banner Style, by switching it from Persistent to Temporary.

What's a "season," and do I have to use them?

A season is just an optional named, color-coded group for your dates (e.g. "Deer season") — it only affects how dates are organized and displayed, not how alarms work. Any date can be left in "No season" and everything still works exactly the same.

What happens to past dates?

Once a date has passed, it moves to "Past dates" automatically and its alarms lock off, since there's nothing left to alert you to. You can still view a past date there, or clear your whole past-dates history at once.

How do I share my dates with someone else?

Pick the dates (or an entire season) you want to send, and HarvestLight hands a one-time link to your device's own share sheet — Messages, Mail, AirDrop, or whatever you choose. Opening that link imports the dates, shooting hours, and season name/color as the recipient's own independent copy. It's a one-time transfer, not a live connection: nothing continues to sync between your copy and theirs afterward, and if they don't have the app yet, the link offers an App Store download.

Is there a limit to how many alarms I can set?

iOS caps every app at a small number of alarms scheduled at once. HarvestLight automatically keeps your soonest upcoming alarms scheduled and rolls the next ones forward as dates pass, so in practice you don't need to think about this — just keep adding dates.

Is my data private?

Yes. HarvestLight has no analytics, no ads, and no third-party tracking of any kind. Your dates and alarms live in your own private iCloud account, and the only time any of it leaves your device is if you deliberately share a link with someone. See the full privacy policy for details.

An alarm didn't go off — what should I check?

  • Make sure notifications are allowed for HarvestLight in Settings → Notifications.
  • Make sure the alarm's start/end toggle is actually on for that date — HarvestLight won't let you set a time that's already passed, so double-check the date and time are both still in the future when you set them.
  • Avoid force-quitting the app from the app switcher; it isn't necessary and background refresh works best when the app hasn't been manually closed.
